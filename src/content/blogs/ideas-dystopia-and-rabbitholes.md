I’ve always been a problem solver. When I was younger, I’d say why can’t we do it this way and ask my mom a billion questions, and if she didn’t know, we’d read about it or Google it together. We’d often find that industries have been doing it that way for years with this core logic behind how things work, or that method failed and brought a better one. While I’d be disheartened that someone did ‘my’ idea before me, I’d also have this sense of accomplishment that I came about the idea on my own and it was successful, or learn from the ones that failed why it did, so I’d be able to think about those flaws too the next time something pops in my head. It’s been a while since I’ve sat with an idea myself before dropping it into AI for easy validation.

We are at the precipice now. We either move on to an era where the value is in ideation and art and creativity, where we take the definition of thinking outside the box to new heights to make lives better, or we move into the dystopia we grew up reading about.

The barrier to code or ship is reducing now, but what does that mean. Just because we can build, doesn’t mean we use computation to create a million versions of the same thing (unless no one’s released a free, good version of it). It means we think bigger, and more different and chase down every what if. But it also means there’s chances that so many other people would’ve thought of or worked on the same idea as you, more or less. But that’s just one part. How do you present the idea, how do you build it, how efficient is it, how do you design and market it, who do you target, what are the guardrails and edge cases, what conditions can it work in that the others can’t, how is it different.

One thing I’ve learnt from reading dystopian novels is that the path of least friction isn’t the path that leads to groundbreaking discoveries. It’s the people who take chances and push their minds to think of things that the others didn’t, or thought couldn’t be done. It’s the ones who break the patterns and stand apart from the flow, while still taking everyone forward with them. And we know that AI inherently is patterns. It’s easy to take problems and ask AI how to solve it or what to build, but know that thousands of people out there are doing the exact same thing for the exact same problem and will get the exact same answer. And ideas, unlike popular opinion, don’t magically fall into someone’s lap, while others are left empty. Ideas come to those who look for them. Because essentially, your brain is a computer of sorts, gathering patterns and messy experiences and emotions of its own. I think good ideas come from the tangled messy web. Maybe AI will get there someday, but today, I’m betting on me. And I think you should too.

Design is everywhere. Problems are everywhere. People are everywhere. Ideas are everywhere. Ideas don’t have to be complex, they can be a simple idea or a small change that leads to a goldmine. You just have to go down enough rabbitholes and wild goose chases to find the ones worth investing in.

Soon you’ll go from ‘hey, why didn’t I think of that’ to presenting ideas that make other people say that, and I hope you’ll tell them this too.
